# RIT Modern Languages and Cultures immersion rules

On 2026-10-11, all eight separately linked public language-immersion pages were read in ordinary Chrome after the current curriculum tables had rendered. The accompanying JSON records each exact official URL, 2026–2027 plan code, all displayed course-code rows, selection rules, footnotes, exclusions, and uncertainty. No hidden curriculum service or restricted academic-catalog route was used. Individual course popups were not opened in this contribution.

The routes are Arabic, Chinese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Russian, and Spanish. They contain 114 course-row observations and 107 distinct codes across routes. Each is a nine-hour optional immersion consisting of three consecutive language courses, or two consecutive language courses plus one culture substitution. This does not make any particular language course universally required in the AI BS. Prior language experience triggers placement; no numeric-code prerequisite chain is invented. Individual course hours are blank in these tables and remain null rather than inferred from the group total.

Two source issues remain explicit. Chinese displays nine hours for the language row and three for the optional culture row, while stating nine total and describing substitution in the overview; these rows must not be added to twelve or silently normalized. Spanish places HIST-130 and HIST-135 in both its language-selection and culture-substitution lists. The observed memberships are preserved, but it is not independently established that these history courses satisfy the language condition.

Arabic cultural options have global-campus restrictions: ANTH-275/365 are Dubai-only; ARTH-549 and HIST-402 are offered only in Kosovo and Dubai and require the named Islamic-art or early-Islamic-history topic. ANTH-255 has route-specific regional conditions in Chinese, Japanese, and Spanish; ENGL-418 requires Russian literature for Russian and Spanish or Latin American literature for Spanish.

Exclusions differ by route. German, Japanese, Russian, and Spanish exclude all global futures majors, whereas Arabic, Chinese, French, and Italian name particular language/region selections. Chinese and Spanish also identify corresponding applied modern language and culture tracks. The precise native-speaker versus fluent-native-speaker qualification is retained. No exemption or approval is presumed. These are ordinary eligibility conditions, not inaccessible-source blockers.
