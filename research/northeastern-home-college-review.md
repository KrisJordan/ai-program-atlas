# Northeastern home-college applicability: three representative degrees

Reviewed 2026-10-10 against the 2026–2027 catalog. Scope: the Boston BS combined degrees in Artificial Intelligence and Business Administration, Biology, and Design. This bounded pass verifies participating colleges and locates the rules whose applicability needs confirmation. It does **not** establish the complete permitted home-college choices for any of the three degrees. All three curriculum audits remain partial; no college policy is newly inherited by the shared family.

## What the public evidence establishes

The [Admissions combined-major directory](https://admissions.northeastern.edu/academics/combined-majors/) explicitly identifies these pairs of associated colleges:

| --- | --- |

| `northeastern-university-artificial-intelligence-and-design-bs-boston-bs-degree` | Khoury / College of Arts, Media and Design |

The directory explains that the college labels identify where the constituent courses are housed. It separately says the college awarding an applicant’s degree appears in their Application Status Check. This is partnership evidence, not a published menu of permitted home-college choices or a universal assignment. No applicant portal or student record was accessed.

The [university combined-major policy](https://catalog.northeastern.edu/undergraduate/academic-policies-procedures/degrees-majors-minors/#text) says a home college is designated and **most** combined majors allow students to choose it. It does not enumerate exceptions or the permitted choices for these three programs. Its general change-of-major process requires consultation with the receiving major and applicable entry/transition criteria; that does not by itself establish the process or eligibility for changing only a combined major’s home college.

## Published college rules, awaiting the applicability link

These are concise research summaries of scoped college policies, not additional unconditional requirements assigned to the three degrees. A program-specific rule can apply independently when its own catalog says so. Publication of a college rule does not establish which students in a combined degree fall under it or whether an approved combined-major exception exists.

| Official college source and locator | Conditions requiring scope confirmation |
| --- | --- |
| [Khoury College](https://catalog.northeastern.edu/undergraduate/computer-information-science/), Academic Progression Standards through Academic Dismissal | Full-time expectation: four courses plus appropriate labs/recitations and 12 earned hours per semester; load exceptions need prior adviser approval. S/U only outside major, minor and NUpath. Graduation overall GPA 2.000. Probation criteria include approved-plan compliance, 12 hours, first-year cumulative GPA 1.800 and major GPA 2.000 at the second term, with later cumulative GPA 2.000. College dismissal and one-term transitional status have separate rules; university dismissal/appeal conditions also apply. |
| [D’Amore-McKim](https://catalog.northeastern.edu/undergraduate/business/), Academic Progression Standards, Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory Option, Taking Courses While on Co-op, External Transfer, Graduation Requirements | Business GPA 2.000; first-year 32 hours (shortfall made up before graduation), then 16 hours per in-school full semester / 8 per summer half-semester for progression. S/U only nonbusiness open electives. One course on co-op; prior written coordinator/adviser approval allows at most two courses or eight hours. External business-core transfer credit capped at 50%; upper-division transfer normally unavailable. Graduation requires prescribed curriculum, overall GPA 2.000 and C average in business courses. |
| [College of Science](https://catalog.northeastern.edu/undergraduate/science/), College Requirements through Academic Dismissal | NUpath and junior-year graduation-clearance meeting. Full-time expectation: four 3–4-credit courses plus labs/recitations and 12 earned hours. Reduced load only for a qualifying final semester with prior adviser request or approved disability accommodation. S/U outside major, minor and NUpath. Graduation major and overall GPA 2.000. First-year semester GPA 1.800 and later cumulative GPA 2.000, plus 12-hour standards. Major GPA below 2.000 from the second full term can trigger major dismissal; transitional status lasts at most two semesters. University dismissal/appeal criteria remain separate. BA-only requirements do not apply to this BS. |
| [CAMD](https://catalog.northeastern.edu/undergraduate/arts-media-design/), Academic Progression Standards, Graduation Clearance, College Requirements | University progression standards plus any additions on the program page; meeting with assigned adviser for graduation clearance, planned during junior year; NUpath. BA-specific requirements are inapplicable to these BS awards. |

The Business/Science differences make guessing consequential: a 12-hour Khoury standing rule cannot silently replace Business’s 16-hour progression criterion, and Science’s two permitted reduced-load circumstances cannot be replaced with unrestricted adviser discretion. The published rules also describe progression, graduation, eligibility and discretionary action separately; they must not be collapsed into extra course totals.

### Exact next evidence needed

For each named Boston BS and catalog year 2026–2027, obtain a public registrar/advising policy or attributable institutional confirmation that states:

1. The allowed home-college designations, any mandatory assignment, and whether the menu differs for applicants, internal changes or entry cohorts.
2. Whether a home-college change is permitted and which approvals/transition criteria apply.
3. Which college progression, grading, transfer, graduation-clearance and dismissal rules govern each designation; whether any combined-degree exceptions override a college rule.
4. The home-college-to-first-year-seminar and co-op-preparation mapping, including INPR 1000 eligibility and whether both colleges’ rules can apply to different parts of the degree.
