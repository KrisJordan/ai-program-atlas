# Case Western language and classics description review

The coordinator read all 50 assigned current Bulletin descriptions on 2026-10-11: 11 GRMN, 11 JAPN, 10 WLIT and 18 CLSC identities. Exact headings, full cleaned description bodies and hashes were checked against the official HTML, with original concise summaries and course-specific classification rationale stored in `atlas/adapters/case_uger_language_reviews.json`.

Sources are the permitted HTTP 200 captures of the 2026–2027 [German](https://bulletin.case.edu/course-descriptions/grmn/), [Japanese](https://bulletin.case.edu/course-descriptions/japn/), [World Literature](https://bulletin.case.edu/course-descriptions/wlit/) and [Classics](https://bulletin.case.edu/course-descriptions/clsc/) descriptions. Raw documents stay outside public commits.

47 courses have explicit cultural, literary, historical or communicative content. CLSC 318 emphasizes archaeological and epigraphic field techniques and is domain practice (applications), with regional history as context. GRMN 314 combines energy technologies/engineering with economic and political questions; WLIT 382 combines translation/book theory with letterpress studio production. Both are explicitly mixed applications/human-context instead of being assigned by department or requirement pool.

WLIT 382 is four Units, unlike the other 49 three-Unit records. It explicitly requires intermediate reading knowledge in a non-English language but no prior printing experience. GRMN 314 limits credit to one of 314 or 315. GRMN 396 and JAPN 397/398 retain senior/declared-major conditions; JAPN 398 also requires a passing JAPN 397 grade. CLSC 381's prerequisite list continues into a second sentence (CLSC 231, 232 and 316) after its senior/declared-major condition; the additional fragment prevents the parser from dropping that continuation. CLSC 199 selects students based on background/interest but explicitly has no language prerequisite. Existing recommendation labels remain advisory, and absent statements remain unknown.

Reciprocal cross-list text is present for several already reviewed historical/literary identities, but no group is inferred or modified by this bounded file. Capstones with explicitly described German/Japanese/ancient-studies subject matter can be classified despite variable individual topics. Eligibility within an AI degree remains a separate policy question; this contribution does not certify the full degree.
