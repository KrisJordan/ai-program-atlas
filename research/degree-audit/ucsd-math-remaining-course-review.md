## Actual review and limits

All 34 matching full blocks and the chapter's section introductions were read. MATH 15A has no exact heading, although several current courses still cite it as preparation. Those mentions do not establish its content, retirement date or replacement, and no current metadata is supplied for that record. The other records had no pre-existing titles, so the exact current headings supply names without inventing a version equivalence.

The descriptions support 33 mathematics classifications and one AI classification, MATH 181D: its actual content is statistical machine learning, feature selection, classification and ensemble methods. MATH 173A principally develops convex optimization and duality with a support-vector-machine application. MATH 155A develops geometric curve representations for graphics. MATH 186 teaches probability and statistics through biological applications. These classifications follow the substantive methods, not the prefix or an isolated application keyword.

MATH 11 is five nominal quarter units; the other 33 approved observations are four. Several have lower or zero credit depending on prior or concurrent enrollment. Course notes preserve those conditions, including the differing prior/concurrent ECON 120A treatment for MATH 180A. Nominal amounts are not unconditional student credit or a degree total. Credit exclusions do not create identity groups. The source explicitly describes 184A → 184 and 21C → 20C renumbering; this helper does not introduce automatic identity mappings or transfer historical content.

The chapter permits equivalent or higher-level prerequisites, while each block can add instructor consent, placement results, grades or recommendations. Exact prerequisite wording is retained; ambiguous Boolean expressions are not silently regrouped. Recommendations for programming, MATH 109 or MATH 102 remain separate from requirements. Offering quarters are tentative.
