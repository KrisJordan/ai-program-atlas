## Content classifications

- **Human context:** CLAS 109/110/120, MGT 18/18GS, LAWS 101, INTL 100, HMNR 100/101 and CCS 123. The classical seminars explicitly study Greek/Latin literature, philosophy, history and religion despite variable topics. CLAS 120 examines race, gender and identity. Human-rights courses and climate policy explicitly focus on historical, legal or political analysis. LAWS 101's annual issue varies, but legal/social perspectives are specified. INTL 100 teaches analytical reading, argument and policy-memo writing.
- **MGT 18/18GS:** actual content centers diversity, cultural/social dynamics and experiences of Asian American and African American team members and leaders. Business success is the context rather than a separately specified technical management methodology. Global Seminar admission changes eligibility, not the described subject.
- **Applications:** MGT 5/102/108R/164/167. Accounting, online commerce, market research, organizational management and entrepreneurship are concrete professional practices. Numerical accounting does not establish general mathematics; online store design/analytics does not establish programming or computing systems. Applied surveys and conjoint analysis support market decisions rather than a general statistics course. Social beneficiaries in entrepreneurship do not automatically make the course primarily social analysis.
- **Unclassified:** INTL 190/190H/196H. These specify seminar, paper, research-design or honors-thesis formats, but no selected substantive topic or particular technical methodology. Department and honors status cannot determine content. This is distinct from the classical/legal seminars whose descriptions establish their actual interpretive subjects.

## Other preparation, grades and scope

- **INTL 100:** requires sophomore standing or above **and** at least one completed quarter of university-level writing. The catalog's Upper Division section location is not substituted for that explicit sophomore threshold.
- **INTL 190:** only International Studies majors, with **C– or better in INTL 100, 101 and 102**. **INTL 190H:** International Studies major, **3.5 major GPA**, department application process, senior standing and 100/101/102; it prepares for 196H and is required for International Studies honors students. It does not repeat 190's C– rule, which is not imported. **INTL 196H:** seniors who completed 190H, with International Studies major status, 3.5 major GPA and department approval. These are source-specific restrictions, not generic access for every AI student.
- **MGT 18GS:** application to the International Center Programs Abroad Office and acceptance into the Global Seminar Program are required; fees may apply. The older pool additionally says department approval. Its omission from this current block does not establish a waiver. MGT 18 and 18GS cannot both earn credit; the exclusion does not create an identity group.
- **MGT 164:** cannot earn credit with 164GS. Its former title, Organizational Leadership, is explicitly recorded without inventing another course code. Upper-division standing is required. MGT 167/102/108R also require upper-division standing.
- **MGT 108R** explicitly states **distance education**. This is source text, not inference from R. The old college-list statement that no prerequisite was stated does not override the current standing rule.
- **CLAS 109/110:** printed prerequisites are LTGK 1/2/3 or equivalent and LTLA 1/2/3 or equivalent, respectively. The source says each may be repeated for credit up to four times as topics vary; repeat wording is retained without conversion into a total number of enrollments. CLAS 120's older pool says upper-division standing, but the current block has no prerequisite clause; the difference is disclosed rather than treated as an automatic waiver.
- **LAWS 101:** upper-division standing or instructor consent. It is required for the Law and Society minor and may count there as History, Philosophy, Political Science or Sociology. Those are minor-applicability classifications, not four identities or an AI-degree requirement. One repeat is allowed, with a maximum total of eight units.
- **CCS 123:** upper-division standing is required. Its current chapter confirms the exact code and climate-policy subject without using a presumed successor program.
