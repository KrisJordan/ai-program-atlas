# RIT anthropology, sociology and global-studies course cards

Reviewed 2026-10-11. This contribution closes the exact **57 assigned identifiers** in the immersion course-content queue: 38 ANTH, 17 SOCI and two INGS. Every complete card was opened through an ordinary public curriculum-table link and read, including its description, prerequisite field when present, credit amount, contact hours, attributes and offering text. No prerequisite-chain expansion, hidden endpoint or alternative access route was used. All 57 cards were accessible.

The actual sources were the 2026–2027 [Cultural Anthropology Immersion](https://www.rit.edu/study/cultural-anthropology-immersion), [Archaeology Immersion](https://www.rit.edu/study/archaeology-immersion) and [Sociology Immersion](https://www.rit.edu/study/sociology-immersion) pages. The manifest binds each course to the precise page where its popup was read. The route sources and titles are individually guarded by the observer. These are optional immersion course observations, not newly mandatory AI-major courses.

Each of the 57 cards independently states **(3 Credits)**. No amount was inferred from the nine-credit immersion total or from lecture contact hours. Semester-hour normalization cites the separately reviewed [RIT D01.4](https://www.rit.edu/policies/d014) convention; the runtime observer deliberately leaves the unit unknown because the cards themselves say only Credits. It accepts a future explicit numeric range as a null scalar with the source amount preserved, and rejects unfamiliar formats. There were no ranges in this actual batch.

Five cards publish a prerequisite/standing field: ANTH-301, ANTH-302, SOCI-301 and SOCI-302 require successful completion of one ANTH, SOCI or INGS course; SOCI-370 restricts enrollment to at least second-year standing. The remaining 52 fields are null, with an explicit source-unreported note. They do not establish unrestricted enrollment. The observer keeps a newly observed prerequisite, changed title, changed description or changed credit value; it does not emit a review audit or category review.

Manual content decisions are **46 human-context, two mathematics, two applications, six mixed and one unclassified**. Each manifest entry has an original summary and individual rationale. Important distinctions include:

- ANTH/SOCI-303 teaches quantitative data analysis and is mathematics. ANTH/SOCI-361 critiques digitization and population metrics, so it is human-context.
- ANTH-215 field techniques and ANTH-415 physical-science archaeological analysis are applications. ANTH-435 explicitly combines biological analysis of remains with mortuary culture and ethics, and remains mixed.
- ANTH-244 combines disability/critical theory with applied ethnographic design. ANTH-328 combines cultural tourism inquiry with site-development evaluation. Both are mixed.
- ANTH-250 has a defined but changing theme set ranging from social developments to human evolution; ANTH-360 combines environmental archaeology and historical human society; ANTH-420 combines historical interpretation with experimental technology reconstruction. Their broad content remains mixed rather than being forced into one subject.
- ANTH-489 leaves its substantive topic to the instructor, department chair, SIS subtitle and syllabus. It remains source-inspected but unclassified; the same topic cannot be repeated. Route-specific topic relevance remains required and is not approved by this review.
- ANTH-375 describes three alternative topics—sovereignty, economies and languages—not a promise that every offering teaches all three. ANTH-420 likewise selects one technological realm.
- The route footnotes restrict ANTH-380, SOCI-235 and SOCI-355 to RIT Global campuses. These limitations remain attached to the observations; readable course content does not create Rochester availability.

Several ANTH/SOCI/INGS cards have identical content and duplicate attribute labels. Every assigned card was independently read. Similarity is not an explicit identity statement, so this contribution creates **no course-identity groups**, splits no slash codes, and promotes no optional course into the fixed set.
