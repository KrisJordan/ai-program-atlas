## Findings

- All 31 cards have actual descriptions. Six print **four credits**: MLAS-201/202 and NASL-180/190/200/210. The other 25 print three. NASL-180's six contact hours are not its credit amount. Course amounts do not change the separately published immersion selection rule.
- Fifteen cards have explicit prerequisite blocks, retained literally. NASL-190 unusually permits NASL-180/equivalent with **C- or D**, or placement score 0, while requiring NTID support. This is not normalized to the later sequence's C-or-better rule.
- NASL-230's preparation names MLAS-401, whereas NASL-240 names MLAS-301; both require at least intermediate ASL in their bodies. Their unparenthesized standing/program/NTID and preparation statements are preserved without inventing Boolean grouping.
- MLAS-351 and MLAS-352 explicitly require ASL fluency and teach in ASL without interpreters. The latter publishes no separate prerequisite block, so that field stays null and the body condition remains in notes. MLAS-201 requires placement testing for students with prior ASL study entering their first RIT ASL course.
- GLPH-105 is restricted to degree-seeking RIT undergraduates. NHSS-251 excludes AOS and CARPRP-UND students. NASL-180/190/200/210 explicitly require NTID support; NASL-180 is an intensive summer pre-college offering for newly admitted deaf and hard-of-hearing students.
- MEDG-106 ends with an unlabelled biology-course parenthetical; this is preserved as preparation evidence, without manufacturing a prerequisite code or operator. GLPH-455 requires its published introductory alternative and includes international travel, additional disclosed costs, orientation and locally coordinated educational work.
- The ASL immersion's ASLINT-BS exclusions are local path rules, not universal course-enrollment prohibitions. LING-449 can repeat only with a different topic; no numeric cap is printed.

## Content review

Twenty-three courses are human-context: actual language/communication analysis and practice, literature, or cultural interpretation. ASL literary creation, narratives and communication drills remain human-context under taxonomy v1; “production” does not by itself mean technical implementation. LING-302 studies linguistic sentence structure, not an evidenced computing algorithm or mathematical proof course. LING-449 remains unclassified because the actual subject is offering-specific.

Four health/biology courses are applications: GLPH-101, GLPH-105, MEDG-106 and NUTR-215. Three are explicitly mixed applications/human_context: DCHP-301 combines religious/ethical analysis and patient-care integration strategies; GLPH-355 combines health/disease content with culture, rights, poverty and institutions; GLPH-455 combines health-system fieldwork and educational delivery with cultural/social-determinant analysis. Each has a course-specific rationale in the manual manifest.
