## Actual content decisions

Manual counts among the 54 described records are **32 applications, ten mathematics, five computing foundations, four AI/ML, one human-context and two mixed**. All 54 headings explicitly state four units. Thirty-six contain an explicit prerequisite field; the other 18 remain unknown, rather than “none.” SIO 20R explicitly states none and is distinguishable from an absent field. Quarter-hour normalization follows the existing UCSD institutional unit convention, not an inference from the numerical amount.

- ECE 15, CENG/NANO 15, SE 9 and BILD 62 teach programming or algorithm construction. Biological or engineering examples do not erase that content.
- ECE 172A explicitly centers machine perception; ECE 175A/175B/176 develop machine learning, graphical inference and deep learning. HDS 110 instead studies biological neural and behavioral development.
- Probability/statistics and experimental-design courses are mathematics even when hosted in engineering, marine biology, psychology or human development. PSYC 70 explicitly covers experimental design, counterbalancing, power and causal inference.
- ASTR 10 is historical and cultural interpretation of astronomy. ASTR 18 is physical science preparation. Introductory psychological mechanisms remain domain applications; a social-psychology host or topic alone does not establish policy or cultural-analysis content.
- ECE 140A and 140B combine software/full-stack construction and commercial product research/development. Their descriptions do not establish a single primary subject, so both remain mixed, with foundations and applications as candidate categories.

Each described entry has an independently authored concise summary and course-specific rationale in the manifest. No classification comes from prefix, pool membership or an unread title. Runtime observations omit both the manual subject category and `category_review`; the primary reviewer must integrate the manifest explicitly.
