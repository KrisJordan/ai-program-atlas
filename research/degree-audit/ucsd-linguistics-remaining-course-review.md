## Findings

The actual content supports three AI observations: LIGN 6 implements natural-language computation in Python, LIGN 165 develops computational linguistic interpretation, and LIGN 167 develops neural models for linguistic data. The other 47 observations concern language, interpretation, communication and cultural literacy and are `human_context`. In particular, LIGN 17's symbolic interpretation and cipher examples do not establish a general programming course. LIGN 4 surveys cognition as linguistic inquiry; an isolated neural-basis topic does not establish a biological laboratory methods course. These decisions use the full descriptions, not identifiers.

Forty-nine headings give four quarter units. LIGN 9GS's actual heading omits a credit amount, which stays null. Its Global Seminars acceptance condition and possible fees are retained. LIGN 6 has no labeled prerequisite; that absence does not prove unrestricted entry. LIGN 167 requires programming ability in addition to its mathematics alternatives.

Heritage-language blocks are distinct variants even where titles coincide. The F/W/P courses can be taken in any order; no mandatory sequence or identity group is created. The Arabic, Korean, Persian, Filipino and Vietnamese descriptions retain their distinct family, media or cultural emphases. All three intermediate Vietnamese descriptions actually use domestic culture, including W and P: suffix patterns are not substituted for content. Cantonese speaking ability is recommended; proficiency and heritage-language preparation remain explicit. Unsuffixed LIHL 112 credit exclusions are preserved without declaring content equivalence.

LISL 1E is entirely in ASL and requires the stated earlier conversation and analysis preparation or equivalent. The chapter's paired 1A–1D conversation/analysis rule does not extend to a fabricated 1EX corequisite. Language placement guidance and the nonannual offering caveat remain visible.

## Adapter and boundaries

`ucsd_linguistics_remaining_reviews.py` guards chapter identity, edition, section introductions, exact expanded or abbreviated identifiers, section membership and each full course body. Source changes require another review. Observations cannot emit manual taxonomy certificates, requirement graphs or identity mappings. The primary adapter composes the observer with the earlier Linguistics helper at the same URL and manually integrates the review metadata.

No seed or ledger is changed by this contribution. The code-only content backlog remains separate from genuine missing-source/version questions. Future checks should review changed blocks and any program-specific applicability rules; this batch establishes course content, not degree eligibility for every student.
