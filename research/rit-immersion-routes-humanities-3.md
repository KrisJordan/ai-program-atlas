## Conditions that change route interpretation

Only **Global Public Health, History, International Relations, Latino/Latina/Latin American Studies, Music, and Philosophy** explicitly require at least one course at the 300 level or above in these reviewed pages. No such condition is automatically added to the other seven routes. No separate preparation-course menu or prerequisite sequence was shown. This observation does not establish that individual courses have no prerequisites. The Spanish-language substitution requires placement for students with previous Spanish study; numeric course codes do not establish a prerequisite chain.

Global Experience requires approval from RIT Global and the RIT General Education Committee, and students must work with their advisor and RIT Global. Every credit needs a substantial approved global or study-away component. Examples include global campuses, study abroad, faculty-led experiences, and study away such as RIT in LA; examples are not a closed course list. The overview calls for general-education elective credit, while the curriculum explicitly accepts GE Electives or Perspectives. Both source statements remain visible; the contribution does not silently narrow or resolve them.

Global Justice requires at least two disciplines. Its overview names global futures, philosophy, political science, and sociology and anthropology majors as needing two courses outside the major; its footnote instead names international and global studies alongside the latter three. No equivalence between the differing program labels is inferred. Legal Studies likewise requires more than one discipline, with two courses outside the major for contributing-program students; its footnote expresses that as at most three credits from the home department. Both formulations are retained.

Global Public Health excludes global public health majors; History excludes history majors; International Relations excludes political science majors; Journalism excludes communication majors in the journalism option; Literature and Media excludes English majors in that concentration; Museum Studies excludes museum studies majors; Philosophy excludes philosophy majors. Latino/Latina/Latin American Studies excludes native Spanish speakers and applied modern language and culture majors in the Spanish track. No approval or exemption is presumed, and these exclusions are not automatically treated as restrictions on an AI-only student.

The Latino route's table combines culture and Spanish-language rows without labeling each row's role. The source-defined three-culture-course rule and optional one-language substitution remain explicit. The contribution does not partition the menu merely from prefixes or titles. ANTH-255 qualifies only for Mesoamerica or Latin America topics. In Health, Culture, and Society, ANTH-250 needs a Hunger and Health topic and ANTH-489 a relevant health/culture/society topic.

Music footnote 2 states that **PRFL-280 through PRFL-285 each earn one semester credit hour and require three semesters of participation to complete one immersion course**. A single ensemble registration therefore cannot be treated as one three-credit immersion selection. The contribution preserves the semester participation condition without inventing an unstated policy about combining different ensembles.

Slash-form labels remain literal: SOCI/ANTH-246, COMM/VISL-390, MUSE/HIST-221, and MUSE/VISL-388. They are neither two-course obligations nor independently established alias groups. The Museum Studies two-MUSE condition remains intact without assuming how registration aliases fulfill it. Source-specific separate rows such as ANTH-295 and SOCI-295 are retained even when titles match.
